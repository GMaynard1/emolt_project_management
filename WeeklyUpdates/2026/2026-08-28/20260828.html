Dissolved Oxygen

Dissolved oxygen levels remain high at sites our partners monitor in coastal Maine. Cape Cod Bay had multiple readings in the (screenshot from Massachusetts DMF’s Cape Cod Bay dashboard below) with low oxygen readings mixed in with normal readings off Scussett Beach and south of Wood End. There was one critically low reading off Barnstable Harbor, but it was mixed in with normal readings, suggesting either a localized hypoxic event or some sort of temporary sensor fouling issue. DMF continues to monitor the situation closely.

On Tuesday, Massachusetts DMF issued the following notice regarding an algal bloom in Cape Cod Bay:

Fixed-gear fishers encouraged to monitor dissolved oxygen in Cape Cod Bay following algal bloom

During the week of August 16 – 22, Massachusetts Water Resources Authority’s (MWRA) water quality monitoring survey in Cape Cod Bay, conducted by Center for Coastal Studies, detected substantial amounts of Karenia mikimotoi in portions of Cape Cod Bay. This is the species of dinoflagellate (a type of phytoplankton, or microscopic algae) that was associated with hypoxic conditions observed in 2019 and 2020 in the southern portion of Cape Cod Bay. Unlike many of the phytoplankton species more commonly found in Cape Cod Bay, K. mikimotoi is adapted to low light conditions and can vertically migrate, allowing it to use the nutrient-rich bottom waters present during late summer. Because of this bloom, we expect that dissolved oxygen (DO) conditions will start to decline. The Division of Marine Fisheries (DMF) will be closely monitoring the DO data for Cape Cod Bay provided by the Cape Cod Bay Study Fleet and eMolt participants to watch for declining DO. Severely hypoxic conditions (DO less than ~2 mg/L) can cause lethargy and death to bottom-dwelling organisms that are sensitive to it, particularly lobsters, crabs, and fish that may be retained in traps and unable to move away from the hypoxic conditions.
We encourage all fixed-gear fishers in Cape Cod Bay to monitor the publicly accessible data from the Study Fleet online. Just click on “map in full screen” to see the most recent conditions displayed. This tool was developed to provide fishers with a means of viewing changing DO conditions to increase awareness of where low DO conditions are occurring and enabling fishers to make informed decisions about where to place their gear to hopefully avoid dead loss in their catch.
If DO conditions deteriorate, DMF will issue a follow-up to this advisory. For more information contact marine.fish@mass.gov.

Bottom Temperature Forecast Performance

Neither forecast model performed particularly well for bottom temperatures this week outside of Southern Maine. Observations Downeast were warmer than forecasted by either model. On the northern edge of Georges, temps were warmer than predicted by Doppio and cooler than predicted by NECOFS. Observations around Cape Cod and in the Mid-Atlantic were mostly cooler than predicted by both models. Observations around Block Island were warmer than forecasted by either model.

American Fisheries Society to Host Panel on Integrating Cooperative Research Into Stock Assessments

Next week is the annual meeting of the American Fisheries Society, a professional association for fisheries scientists. On Tuesday morning from 0800-0945, AFS’s Cooperative Research with Stakeholders Section has convened a panel of experts to discuss best practices for integrating cooperative research into stock assessments. To join virtually please use the link below the session description.

Cooperative Fisheries Research Pathways and Challenges: 8:00 AM – 9:45 AM Cooperative research can fill critical data gaps at management-relevant scales, yet integrating stakeholder collected observations into formal stock assessments remains challenging.
