eMOLT Update 2026-05-08

Temps are coming up around the region with parts of southern Maine and the area around Block Island up in the 40s, although cold water is hanging on Downeast and along the New Hamshire Seacoast. Thanks to Captain John aboard the F/V Direction for taking some time out of his maintenance work to chat with Huanxin and me this week. While much of the conversation was focused on how we’d rig up an eMOLT system aboard his vessel, we also touched on science communication, and how the Northeast Fisheries Science Center gets information back to all of you. In particular, the spring Bottom Trawl Survey is out right now aboard the R/V Pisces. In the interest of avoiding gear conflict with fixed gear fishermen, they’re looking for better ways to communicate their survey station locations. If you have ideas, feel free to reach out.

This week, the eMOLT fleet recorded 122 tows of sensorized fishing gear totaling 2160 sensor hours underwater.

FISHBOT bottom temperature records from the past week. The data are available on the Commercial Fisheries Research Foundation ERDDAP and an interactive visualization is available at the Cape Cod Ocean Watch dashboard hosted by Woods Hole Oceanographic Institution. FISHBOT aggregates data provided by participants in eMOLT, the CFRF Lobster and Jonah Crab Research Fleet, the CFRF Shelf Research Fleet, the Cape Cod Commercial Fishermen’s Alliance Cape Cod Oceanographic Research Fleet, the Maine Coast Fishermen’s Association Fisheries Ocean Data Program, MassDMF Cape Cod Bay Study Fleet, the Northeast Fisheries Science Center Study Fleet, and the Northeast Fisheries Science Center Ecosystem Monitoring Surveys

Bottom Temperature Forecast Performance

Both models performed well this week around the region, although they differed quite a bit around Block Island. While temps were cooler than NECOFS forecasted, they were warmer than Doppio forecasted. Temps were slightly warmer than both models predicted on the western edge of the Great South Channel and cooler than both models predicted southeast of Penobscot Bay.

Comparisons between bottom temperatures predicted by two ocean forecasting models and observations from the eMOLT fleet. Blue dots show where the observations were cooler than the forecast and red dots show where the observations were warmer than the forecast. White dots show areas where the observations and forecasts agreed. On the left is the comparison with the Doppio model and on the right is the comparison with the NECOFS model (GOM7).

Other happenings

  • We are still looking for research partners (commercial fishermen or fish dealers) for our mackerel biological sampling program. If you catch and land mackerel in the Gulf of Maine, or if you process mackerel shoreside and are interested in participating, please contact katherine.viducic@noaa.gov for more information. Compensation is available.

  • Want an eMOLT system for your boat or know somebody else who does? Check out our online eMOLT System Request form to get on the wait list. Being able to break this list down by gear type and geographic area is helpful when we apply for funding to expand the program.
